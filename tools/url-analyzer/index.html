Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers about URL privacy, phishing detection, risk scoring, security limitations and how the ThreatHawk URL Analyzer works.

Is the URL I enter uploaded or stored by ThreatHawk?

The URL Analyzer performs its structural and heuristic checks directly through the analysis workflow in your browser. ThreatHawk does not require an account and does not intentionally save URLs in a personal history or user profile. However, if an external reputation service is enabled in the analyzer, the submitted URL may be sent to that security provider for reputation checking. Avoid entering private dashboard links, password-reset links, authentication tokens or other confidential addresses.

How does ThreatHawk detect a possible phishing URL?

ThreatHawk examines multiple signals inside the submitted address. These can include insecure protocols, suspicious words, misleading subdomains, excessive URL length, unusual symbols, encoded characters, redirect parameters, IP-based hosts and possible brand impersonation. Each detected signal contributes to the final risk score and appears in the Security Findings section so the result remains understandable rather than behaving like a mysterious black box.

Can the URL Analyzer guarantee that a website is safe?

No security analyzer can guarantee that a website is completely safe. A malicious page may be newly created, temporarily inactive, carefully disguised or not yet included in public threat databases. ThreatHawk should be treated as an investigation and awareness tool, not as a replacement for browser protection, antivirus software, safe-browsing services or professional security analysis. A low score means fewer suspicious indicators were found, not that the website has received permanent approval from the internet safety council, which thankfully does not exist.

What does brand impersonation mean?

Brand impersonation occurs when an attacker creates a domain or URL that resembles a trusted company, service or financial institution. The attacker may use misspelled names, extra characters, deceptive subdomains or visually similar letters. For example, a suspicious domain may contain the name of a known service without actually belonging to that company. ThreatHawk checks for these patterns and reports them when the URL appears designed to create false trust.

Why can a legitimate URL receive a medium or high risk score?

Risk scoring is based on suspicious characteristics, not on a declaration that the website is malicious. Legitimate websites sometimes use long tracking links, multiple redirect parameters, unusual subdomains, encoded values or brand names inside their paths. These patterns are also commonly used in phishing campaigns, so the analyzer may flag them for review. Always examine the listed findings and confirm the real domain owner before deciding whether a result is a false positive.

Does the analyzer check SSL certificates and HTTPS?

ThreatHawk checks whether the submitted address uses HTTP or HTTPS and treats HTTPS as a positive security signal. However, HTTPS alone does not prove that a website is trustworthy because attackers can also obtain valid SSL certificates. Unless a dedicated certificate-inspection feature is shown in the results, the analyzer does not verify certificate ownership, expiration dates, issuing authorities or the complete TLS configuration of the remote server.

Can I analyze shortened or redirected URLs?

You can submit shortened URLs, but the visible shortened address may not reveal the final destination. ThreatHawk can inspect the structure of the URL you enter and identify suspicious patterns within it. If redirect-chain inspection is not available in the current version, the analyzer may not automatically follow every redirect to examine the final landing page. Treat unknown shortened links carefully, especially when they arrive through unsolicited messages, emails or social-media posts.

Is the ThreatHawk URL Analyzer free to use?

Yes. The URL Analyzer is free to use and does not require account registration, a subscription or a payment. You can inspect website addresses, review URL components, examine security findings and view the generated risk score directly in a modern web browser. Some future external reputation features may depend on third-party service limits, but the core ThreatHawk analyzer remains available without a paid account.

Does the URL Analyzer work on mobile devices?

Yes. The interface is designed to work on desktop computers, laptops, tablets and smartphones. The navigation, input workspace, risk assessment, findings and FAQ section automatically adapt to smaller screens. For easier inspection of very long URLs and technical details, a larger screen may still be more comfortable because apparently URLs were designed with no concern for human eyesight.
